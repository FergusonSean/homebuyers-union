An owner whose mortgage is paid off at 55 spends their remaining decades in free housing — paying only taxes and maintenance. What your money actually does

Donor capital has two jobs: it puts money directly into the groups that need it most, and it keeps the nonprofit running.

1. It funds capital that goes into member groups. Most of every donation becomes a capital contribution into a member group's LLC — real money added to the pool a group uses to buy homes. This is allocated by income: the groups with the lowest average member income are served first, so the households furthest from ownership get the most help.

2. It keeps the nonprofit running. The remainder funds operating costs — the staffing, legal work, and infrastructure the organization needs to exist. This share is capped: only a limited portion of donation money can ever go to operations, so the majority always reaches members.

The split

The exact percentages depend on the operations cap and the income-allocation rule, both still being finalized. What is already decided is the shape: a capped slice for operations, and the rest flowing into group capital, lowest-average-income groups first.

No returns. No equity. Just impact.

We are going to say this plainly because it matters: this is a charitable gift.

Donors receive no financial return. No equity stake. No claim on any property. No ownership interest in the organization or its assets. When the union dissolves and houses are gifted to members, donors receive nothing material.

This is not a crowdfunded real estate investment. It is not a community investment vehicle. It is not a structured financial product with downside protection. It is a donation to a charitable organization whose mission is to help people own homes.

We say this not as a warning but as a point of pride. The Homebuyers Union exists to serve its members. The only people it is structured to benefit are the families going through the program. Donors make that possible — and that is the entire return.

If you need your money to come back, this is not the right vehicle. If you want to give it — cleanly, directly, with real impact you can follow — you're in the right place.

How funds are governed

A capped share funds operations; the rest becomes member group capital. A fixed rule — not a person — decides which groups are served first.

Operations are capped; members get the rest

The nonprofit's operating costs — staffing, legal, infrastructure — are funded from donations, but only up to a fixed cap. Beyond that cap, every additional dollar goes to member group capital. You are never funding an open-ended overhead budget.

A rule decides who gets help, not a person

There is no grants committee, no application review, no staff member weighing one family's circumstances against another's. Group capital is allocated by a fixed rule — average member income, lowest first — so the groups furthest from ownership are served first. The priority is set by the rule, not by anyone's discretion.
